# map-procedural-generation — rules

Combine with the root CLAUDE.md. This is the **content / assembly** stage: it consumes the base terrain from `../map-procedural-generation-terrain/` (THE standalone base terrain generator) and CHUNKS its big `all.tmx` into per-map **zstd**-base64 `.tmx`, then adds cities/roads, reciprocal borders + doors, NPCs/trainers/signs, wild-encounter tables and interiors (heal / shop / gym). Full on-disk spec = `target.md`; per-datapack tile/item/skin config = `settings*.xml` + `DATAPACK-CONFIG.md`; what is left to do, and which part needs DRAWN art instead of code = `TODO.md`.

* Output is a STAGING tree at `<binary>/dest/map/main/official/` — NEVER the source datapack (`CatchChallenger-datapack/` is read-only). `cities.txt`/`dialog.txt`/`template/`/`skin/` load from the BINARY directory (CMake stages them there); settings come from `settings.xml` (or `--config settings-reference.xml`).
* **The output is ONE self-contained map LABEL: it SHIPS its own `tileset/`.** `dest/map/main/official/` is copied under `map/main/<label>/` of a datapack (`install-generated.py <dest> --datapack <dir> [--label generated]`, which refuses to install while a reference escapes the label, and skips the `all.tmx` debug dump). So every written reference is `../tileset/x.tsx` — NEVER `../../../tileset/` (the datapack `map/tileset/` is read-only AND owns none of the gym/heal/shop/house sheets: that copy loaded with no tileset at all). `dest/map/tileset/` + `dest/map/main/tileset/` stay the RUN-STAGING POOL (`--datapack` fills them, `stageTemplateTileset` adds the template ones); `LoadMap::shipTileset` copies out of the pool at WRITE time, so an unused tileset is never shipped. `check-generated.py` enforces it.
* **RIGID rectangular grid-aligned features** (root + terrain CLAUDE.md): the terrain generator shapes the grass/feature zones; FILL them solid, NEVER thin a zone with per-cell noise to look "natural" (the `doc/mapping/Badmap.png` mistake — reverted once already).
* **Build/run:** `cmake -S . -B <build> && cmake --build <build> && <build>/map-procedural-generation --datapack <CatchChallenger-datapack>` (out-of-tree). the `map-procedural-generation-runtime` target (always built) stages `template/ skin/ tileset/` and the txt/xml inputs next to the binary — a POST_BUILD command would only run when the binary relinks, so a template edit would never reach the build dir, `--datapack` stages the datapack tilesets into `dest/map/` — a fresh build directory generates with that one command. Without `--datapack` it ABORTS unless `<binary>/dest/map/tileset/` already holds the `.tsx`+`.png` the chosen `settings.xml` names (it reads each tileset's tile count for `firstgid`). ~23 s for 400 cities / 2500 maps; repeated runs are byte identical.
* **VERIFY by rendering** (vision): maps are **zstd** base64 (`Base64Zstandard`, everywhere: chunks, interiors, all.tmx) — a renderer without zstd shows all-black. Use a zlib tileset renderer that follows each map's own relative `<tileset source>` (they resolve inside the label, `dest/map/main/official/tileset/`). A city must read as a town (HEAL/SHOP/gym buildings, grid trees); roads carry rigid grass blocks.
* **Content quality:** NPC/trainer `<name>` come from an invented neutral name pool in `botStepXml` (LoadMapRoad.cpp), NOT the numeric bot id; flavor NPC lines come from `dialog.txt` (original themed sentences, not Lorem-Ipsum). `target.md` §2.1 = the region / location / `building/` naming taxonomy (real name → type fallback `city-N`/`cave-N`/`road-N`; isolated interiors under one `building/`; multi-region split by name/graph/`region-N`).
* **NEVER any franchise wording** (Pokemon/Pokemart/Nintendo/a real character name…) in a generated text: the world is ORIGINAL. Generic English words are fine (Mart, Hospital, Gym). `npcfill.py` filters the model output and `check-generated.py` re-checks the whole tree.

# Building templates (`template/<group>/`)

Every city building comes from a template FOLDER, discovered on disk (`LoadMapBuilding.cpp`), never hard-coded: one exterior tmx (brushed on the city ground) + `floor-N.tmx` interiors + `floor-N.xml` **content skeleton**. A group is either one folder per variant (`sea-city/1`..`7`) or the files directly (`shop-small`).
* Groups used: `heal-{small,medium,big}` and `shop-{small,medium,big}` picked by CITY SIZE, `gym-building` (non-small cities, keeps its historical shape: the template's fight bot is the LEADER, `[building] gymTrainers` extra trainers are injected on free cells and removed from the shared template afterwards), and one `*-city` STYLE group per city for the filler houses (`[building] cityStyleTerrains` matches the terrain, else random fallback). Output names: `heal/shop/gym/house-N.tmx`+`.xml` (multi-floor → folder + `floor-N`).
* The skeleton xml gives the bot ids and their STEP TYPES (`text`/`shop`/`sell`/`heal`/`warehouse`/`fight`) and its `<a href>` links (kept: they are the bot flow); every CONTENT is regenerated per city — names, lines, shop catalogue (grows with the city level), fight teams (city monster pool + gym type at the city level).
* The generator OWNS the door wiring: it creates the missing exterior door / interior exit and always rewrites `map`+`x`/`y` (several shipped templates point at a spawn outside their interior). Exterior door object = the walkable DOORSTEP cell (the engine door tile is the collision one above it, object Y carries the -1); interior exit = bottom row, landing above it.
* It also repairs, with a warning: bot object without `type="bot"`, bot object outside the `Object` group (the ONLY group the engine reads), duplicated bot id, skin absent from `skin/` (→ role skin of settings), tileset not staged (copied into `dest/map/tileset/`).
* `template-check.py [--fix]` is the template repair tool — run it after editing `template/`. It fixes IN THE FILES: unresolved/duplicated tileset lines, missing `type="bot"`, bot objects outside the `Object` group, bots walled in or with a skin the datapack lacks, duplicated bot ids, missing door/exit objects and wrong door targets, an interior with no NPC at all, the missing `indoor` property, and the skeleton debris (`zone=`, a `backgroundsound` that does not exist, a `<bot>` with no object). `check-generated.py <dest>` validates the OUTPUT (teleports resolve and land on a walkable cell, interiors have a way out, bot ids match, no franchise wording).
* **NPC lines: `npc-slots.json`, tracked by git.** The generator writes `dest/npc-requests.json` (every text it produced + its context + its BUCKET) and READS `npc-slots.json` (staged next to the binary by CMake): bucket key → reviewed lines, picked deterministically. `python3 npcfill.py [--time-limit <min>] [--only <key>] [--check]` fills the missing buckets with the local Ollama model (`mistral-small-4:119b`) and rewrites that file only — the human reviews the git diff, rebuilds, regenerates. ~175 buckets for a world, resumable (a bucket with ≥6 lines is never asked again). NPC **names stay hand-written** (`--names` to ask anyway: a model returns names of existing games far too often). Without any bucket the maps still read well: `dialog.txt` (70 original lines) + 5-6 written lines per role. Bucket key (identical in `LoadMapBuilding.cpp` and `npcfill.py`): `<group>|<role>|<field>|<level tier>` +`|<size>` for a house +`|<element>` for a gym leader.
* **HARD RULE — never generate, recolour or modify a TILE, a tileset or a building facade.** The art is drawn by the owner; a tileset exists to be REUSED by many buildings, so adding a generated sheet per variant is wrong by design, and a hue rotation is not architecture (a purple desert house). The generator only ASSEMBLES the drawn templates. More visual variety = the owner draws a new `template/<style>/<n>/` folder; the code side is already ready for it (folders are discovered on disk). A recolour attempt was reverted for exactly this reason.
* **City/gym/cave settings:** `[city]` — avenue path-terrain is DERIVED from `template/city-big.tmx`/`city-medium.tmx` (Walkable fill = path, OnGrass 3x3 ring = border; edit the tmx to retheme; `useAsBase` brushes the tmx itself centered), per-type `signTiles` list (one sign style per city, signs at the avenue entrances, re-asserted post-vegetation so nothing hides them). Big city: STREET-FRONT LOTS filled center-out on both avenue frontages (doored buildings only on the door-facing side, door-front tiles validated+reserved), most houses doorless facades, plaza patches. `[building] cityTypeTerrains="type->terrainKeyword,..;.."` — every CITY takes the element type whose terrain keywords match its surroundings (water by the sea, stone in the mountains, plant in the grass, ice on snow...), per-type counts quota-balanced; the GYM type always matches the city type. `[building] gymTypes="type[:#color]->Mon1,..;.."` — typed gyms: part of the trainer teams from the type pool, and `tileset/gym.png` (lower parts = recolor mask at +128px) recolored per type-color into `dest/map/tileset/gym-<type>.*` (`typeXml` can point at the datapack `monsters/type.xml` for colors). `[road] cave\*` — every selected road chunk becomes a cave; NO artificial cliff is ever drawn (owner: ugly). Per connection, the mouth is embedded in the FIRST natural cliff on the straight approach line from the border (`entranceTile` facing bottom / `entranceTopTile` terra-19 from the top side), else a small 3x3 rock OUTCROP (the `wallTile` block) stands on the flat ground; the approach band is cleared+vegetation-masked and the surrounding terrain/vegetation seals naturally. The corridor is the separate `<chunk>-cave[-N].tmx` `type="cave"` interior: ring-only collision (overworld mountain ring `region\mountain_tile/tsx`), NEVER touches the map border; exit DOORS are push-teleports ON the cliff at the accessible EXTREMITY — top side `exitTopTile` one above a walk-up gap at the TOPMOST reachable collision, other sides `exitBottomTile` at the BOTTOMMOST (the sealed border line counts); each side connects to a RANDOM floor (crossing may require the `stairDown/UpTile` stairs, "go deeper to exit"), +2 monster levels & luck rotation per floor, `itemPercent`/`items`/`itemTile` ground items, trainers inside. The whole plan lives in `LoadMapAll::cavePlans` (decided at selection time). `[wildMonsters] <id>\name` — generated xml references monsters by lowercase NAME; `minLevel==maxLevel` collapses to `level=`. `start.xml` gets one `<start>` per city in the lowest 10% of the level range (lowest = `Normal`; others = city-name ids, active once matching profiles exist in `player/start.xml`).
